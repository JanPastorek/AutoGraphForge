In graph theory, the terms **radius**, **Roman domination number**, and **rad(G)** are defined as follows:

### 1. Radius, rad(G)
The **radius** of a graph $G$, denoted by **$\text{rad}(G)$**, is defined as the minimum eccentricity among all vertices in the graph[1][2]. 
*   **Eccentricity $\epsilon(u)$:** For a vertex $u$, this is the maximum distance from $u$ to any other vertex $v$ in the graph ($ \epsilon(u) = \max_{v \in V} d(u, v) $)[3][2].
*   **Mathematical Definition:** $\text{rad}(G) = \min \{ \epsilon(u) : u \in V(G) \}$[1][4].
*   A vertex $v$ is called a **central vertex** (or center) if its eccentricity is equal to the radius of the graph[5][4].

### 2. Roman Domination Number, $\gamma_R(G)$
**Roman domination** is a graph-theoretical model based on a historical military strategy[6]. 
*   **Roman Dominating Function (RDF):** A function $f: V \to \{0, 1, 2\}$ such that every vertex $u$ with $f(u) = 0$ is adjacent to at least one vertex $v$ with $f(v) = 2$[3][7][8].
*   **Roman Domination Number:** The weight of an RDF is the sum of the values assigned to all vertices ($\sum_{v \in V} f(v)$)[3][9]. The Roman domination number, denoted as **$\gamma_R(G)$**, is the minimum weight among all possible Roman dominating functions on the graph $G$[3][10].

### Relationship in Literature
Research often investigates the relationship between these parameters[11]. For example:
*   Some studies compare the Roman domination number with the radius of a graph to establish bounds[12][11].
*   The radius is also used in specific graph parameters like "Global Distance Roman Domination," where the bounds for the domination number are often expressed in terms of $\text{rad}(G)$ (e.g., cases where the value changes based on whether a distance $k$ is greater than or equal to the radius)[13][14].
