In graph theory, the **Roman domination number** ($\gamma_R(G)$) is a variation of the classical domination number where vertices are labeled with 0, 1, or 2 such that every vertex labeled 0 is adjacent to at least one vertex labeled 2, and the total weight (sum of labels) is minimized[1][2].

Researchers frequently study the relationships between the Roman domination number and other fundamental graph invariants such as **diameter**, **radius**, and **girth** to establish bounds and characterize specific graph classes[3][4][5].

### Key Relationships and Bounds

*   **Diameter ($\text{diam}(G)$):** The diameter—the maximum distance between any two vertices—is often used to bound the Roman domination number[6][7]. For instance, research has shown that for connected graphs with a sufficiently large Roman domination number (e.g., $\gamma_R(G) > 3$), there are upper bounds on the diameter[8]. Conversely, studies explore how the diameter and other parameters constrain the minimum weight of a Roman dominating function[3][4].
*   **Girth ($\text{gir}(G)$):** The girth—the length of the shortest cycle in a graph—is another parameter used to refine bounds on $\gamma_R(G)$[9][7]. Specific results exist for graphs with restricted girth, such as triangle-free graphs (girth $\geq 4$) or graphs with higher girth, where the Roman domination number is analyzed in relation to the graph's structure[10].
*   **Radius ($\text{rad}(G)$):** While less commonly paired directly with Roman domination than diameter, the radius (the minimum eccentricity of any vertex) is often investigated alongside diameter in extremal graph theory problems[7][11]. Bounds for domination-related parameters frequently incorporate radius to describe the "spread" of the graph[12][11].

### Research Context
*   **Extremal Problems:** Much of the literature focuses on finding graphs that satisfy equality in certain bounds (e.g., $\gamma_R(G) = n - \Delta + 1$) or characterizing graphs where the Roman domination number behaves in a specific way relative to their diameter and girth[1][10][8].
*   **General Inequalities:** For any connected graph, the Roman domination number is bounded by the order of the graph ($n$), specifically $2 \leq \gamma_R(G) \leq n$ (for $n \geq 3$)[1][13]. More precise bounds often involve the maximum degree ($\Delta$), minimum degree ($\delta$), and the aforementioned distance-based parameters[8][14].

If you are looking for a specific theorem or inequality linking these four parameters, papers by authors such as **B.P. Mobaraky**, **S.M. Sheikholeslami**, or those focusing on **extremal graph theory** are the primary sources for these mathematical relationships[3][5].
