In graph theory, **Roman domination** and the **radius** of a graph are distinct concepts that are often studied in relation to one another but do not constitute a single standard term like "Roman domination radius"[1][2].

### Key Concepts

*   **Roman Domination Number ($\gamma_R(G)$):** This is a graph invariant defined by a function $f: V \to \{0, 1, 2\}$ such that every vertex $u$ with $f(u) = 0$ is adjacent to at least one vertex $v$ with $f(v) = 2$[3][4]. The weight of the function is the sum of the labels, and $\gamma_R(G)$ is the minimum weight among all such functions[5][4].
*   **Radius of a Graph ($\text{rad}(G)$):** The radius is the minimum eccentricity among all vertices in a graph[6][4]. The eccentricity of a vertex is the maximum distance from that vertex to any other vertex in the graph[6][4].

### Relationship in Research
While there is no singular definition for a "Roman domination radius," researchers frequently investigate the **relationship between the Roman domination number and the radius** of a graph[1][2]. 

Studies in this area typically focus on:
*   **Bounding/Comparing:** Establishing upper and lower bounds for the Roman domination number in terms of the graph's radius (e.g., comparing $\gamma_R(G)$ with $\text{rad}(G)$ for specific graph classes like trapezoid graphs or trees)[1][2].
*   **Distance-k Roman Domination:** Some research expands the definition of Roman domination to "distance-$k$" variants, where the requirement is that a vertex with label 0 must be within distance $k$ of a vertex with label 2[6][7]. This links the concept of "distance" directly to the Roman domination function, which is distinct from the graph-theoretic radius but uses similar distance-based logic[6][7].
