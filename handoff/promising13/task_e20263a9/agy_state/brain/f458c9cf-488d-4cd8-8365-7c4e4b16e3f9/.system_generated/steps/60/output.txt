The study of the relationship between the **domination number** ($\gamma(G)$) and the **radius** ($\text{rad}(G)$) of a connected graph $G$ is a well-established topic in graph theory[1][2]. Researchers have focused on finding upper bounds for the domination number using graph parameters such as the radius, diameter, order, and minimum degree[1][3][4].

### Key Relationships and Bounds

*   **General Bounds:** The domination number $\gamma(G)$ is often compared to the radius and diameter of a graph[1][4]. Specifically, research has been conducted to derive "tight upper bounds on the domination number" by considering these parameters alongside others, such as the order $n$ and minimum degree $\delta(G)$[1][4].
*   **Specific Parameter Studies:** 
    *   Some studies explore variations like the **radius domination number** ($\gamma_{rd}(G)$), where a dominating set $S$ is required to satisfy a distance condition related to $\text{rad}(G)$ (e.g., for every $v \in V-S$, there exists $u \in S$ such that $d(u, v) = \text{rad}(G)$)[5][6].
    *   Other research has investigated the **connected domination number** ($\gamma_c(G)$) and its relationship to the radius and diameter of a graph, often providing bounds for specific classes of graphs[7][8].
*   **Existing Literature:** Notable work in this area includes papers that establish and strengthen bounds on domination parameters[1][4]. For instance, results by Henning and Mukwembi have served as a foundation for further research into bounding the domination number using the radius and minimum degree[1][4].

### Summary of Research Context
If you are looking for a specific inequality or proof, the literature typically addresses these bounds through the following approaches:
1.  **Construction-based proofs:** Showing that specific upper bounds hold even for highly connected graphs[1][4].
2.  **Comparative studies:** Comparing different types of domination (e.g., Roman, total, or distance-$k$ domination) with the radius of the graph[9].
3.  **Algorithmic/Complexity perspectives:** Discussing how these bounds relate to the structural properties of graphs, such as trees or graphs with specific degree constraints[10][4].

For precise, recently established upper bounds, the paper *"Radius, diameter, domination number, order and minimum degree"* by P. Mafuta (2024) is a relevant resource that discusses these specific parameters in detail[1][4].
