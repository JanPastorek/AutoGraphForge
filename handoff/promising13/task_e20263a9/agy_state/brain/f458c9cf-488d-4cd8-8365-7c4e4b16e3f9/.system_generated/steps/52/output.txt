### Context and Usage
*   **Nonsplit Roman Domination:** In studies investigating the nonsplit Roman domination number, researchers have established results relating this parameter to the graph's radius and diameter[1][2]. Specifically, a known corollary states:

*   **Radius and Domination:** The radius of a graph, $\text{rad}(G)$, is defined as the minimum eccentricity among all vertices in $G$[3][4]. It is frequently compared with various domination numbers (including the standard Roman domination number $\gamma_R(G)$ or $\gamma_{gR}(G)$) to establish upper or lower bounds for specific classes of graphs[5][6][7].

While there is no single universal theorem stating $\gamma_R(G) = \text{rad}(G) + 1$, the terms often appear together in theorems that bound domination parameters in terms of the graph's radius, diameter, or other distance-based metrics[1][5][2].
