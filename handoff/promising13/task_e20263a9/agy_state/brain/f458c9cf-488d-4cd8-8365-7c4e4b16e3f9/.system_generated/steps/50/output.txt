In graph theory, the Roman domination number $\gamma_R(G)$ of a connected graph $G$ is often studied in relation to its structural parameters, such as the diameter $\text{diam}(G)$. 

Research into bounds for the Roman domination number has established that it can be bounded from below using the diameter of the graph[1][2][3]. Specifically, one known lower bound is:

$$\gamma_R(G) \geq \left\lceil \frac{\text{diam}(G) + 2}{2} \right\rceil$$

This result appears in literature exploring various bounds on Roman domination, often alongside considerations of other graph invariants like the girth or maximum degree[1][4][3]. Other studies have also examined lower bounds for different variants of Roman domination (such as outer-independent or distance Roman domination) in relation to the order, maximum degree, and diameter of graphs[5][6][7].
