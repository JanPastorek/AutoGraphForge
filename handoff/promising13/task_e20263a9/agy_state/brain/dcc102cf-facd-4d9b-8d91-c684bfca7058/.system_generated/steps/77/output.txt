The research article titled **"Comparison of Roman domination number, Total domination number and distance-2 domination number with radius of a trapezoid graph TG"**, published in the *International Journal of Scientific Development and Research* (IJSDR) in January 2021 (Volume 6, Issue 1), investigates the relationships between these specific graph parameters[1][2].

### Key Findings and Concepts
*   **Graph Class:** The study focuses on **trapezoid graphs**, which are intersection graphs of trapezoids defined by two horizontal lines ($L_1$ and $L_2$)[3][4].
*   **Objective:** The paper compares the **Roman domination number**, **total domination number**, and **distance-2 domination number** against the **radius** of the graph[1][5]. 
*   **Relationship:** One of the specific results mentioned in the paper is the proof that the Roman domination number of a trapezoid graph is greater than its radius[2]. For instance, in an example provided in the study, a graph with a radius of 3 was found to have a Roman domination number of 5 (satisfying the $5 > 3$ relationship)[2].
*   **Definitions:**
    *   **Roman Domination Number ($\gamma_{Rom}(G)$):** The minimum weight of a Roman dominating function on a graph[6].
    *   **Radius (rad(G)):** The minimum eccentricity among all vertices in the graph, representing the distance from the "most central" vertex to the farthest vertex[7][8].
    *   **Total Domination Number ($\gamma_t(G)$):** The minimum cardinality of a set of vertices such that every vertex in the graph is adjacent to at least one vertex in the set[9].

This work contributes to the broader field of domination in graphs, where researchers often seek to establish bounds and inequalities between different domination-related parameters and structural metrics like the radius and diameter[1][10].
