In graph theory, **Roman domination** and the **radius** of a graph are distinct concepts that are often studied in relation to one another[1], but they are not the same parameter.

### Key Definitions
*   **Roman Domination:** A Roman dominating function on a graph $G = (V, E)$ is a function $f: V \to \{0, 1, 2\}$ such that every vertex $u$ with $f(u) = 0$ is adjacent to at least one vertex $v$ with $f(v) = 2$[2][3]. The **Roman domination number**, denoted by $\gamma_R(G)$, is the minimum weight ($\sum_{v \in V} f(v)$) among all such functions[2][3].
*   **Radius:** The radius of a graph, denoted by $rad(G)$, is the minimum eccentricity among all vertices in the graph[2][4]. The eccentricity of a vertex $v$ is the maximum distance from $v$ to any other vertex in the graph[2][5].

### Relationship Between the Two
While there is no single "Roman domination radius," researchers often analyze how the **Roman domination number relates to the radius** of specific graph classes[1]. 

*   **Comparative Studies:** Various research papers compare parameters like the Roman domination number, total domination number, and distance-2 domination number against the radius of graphs (such as trapezoid graphs or trees) to establish bounds and inequalities[6][1].
*   **Distance Roman Domination:** There is a specific variation called **distance Roman domination** (or $k$-distance Roman domination)[7]. In this variant, the requirement is modified: for every vertex $v$ with $f(v) = 0$, there must be a vertex $u$ with $f(u) = 2$ at a distance $d(u, v) \leq k$[7]. This concept explicitly incorporates distance (which is the foundational metric for determining the radius) into the definition of the domination function[7].

If you are looking for a specific theorem or bound involving both, it is likely within the context of **distance-constrained domination** or bounds on the Roman domination number based on the graph's diameter or radius[7][1].
