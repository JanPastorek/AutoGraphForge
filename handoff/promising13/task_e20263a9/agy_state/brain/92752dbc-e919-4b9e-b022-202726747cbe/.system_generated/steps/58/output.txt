The relationship between the **diameter** ($\text{diam}(G)$) and the **domination number** ($\gamma(G)$) of a graph is a significant topic in domination theory. While there is no single simple formula that bounds $\gamma(G)$ solely by $\text{diam}(G)$ for all graphs, there are established upper bounds and specific behaviors for graphs with small diameters.

### Key Insights and Bounds

*   **Diameter 2 Graphs:**
    *   For graphs with diameter 2, the domination number $\gamma(G)$ can be constrained more tightly than in general graphs.
    *   Research has shown that for a graph $G$ of diameter 2 and order $n$, the domination number is generally bounded by $\gamma(G) \le \lfloor n/4 \rfloor$ for sufficiently large $n$ (specifically $n \ge 24$)[1].
    *   Earlier results provided bounds of $O(n^{3/4})$, which have been improved over time to results such as $\gamma(G) \le (\frac{1}{2} + o(1))\sqrt{n \log n}$ as $n \to \infty$[2].
*   **Diameter 3 Graphs:**
    *   In graphs with diameter 3, it has been shown that the domination number can be as large as $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor$, but it does not exceed this value[2].
*   **General Context:**
    *   In the absence of diameter constraints, the most well-known general upper bound for a connected graph with no isolated vertices is $\gamma(G) \le n/2$[3][4][5].
    *   The domination number is a fundamental graph invariant[6], and finding it is NP-complete for general graphs[3][7]. Consequently, researchers often look for bounds based on other parameters like maximum degree ($\Delta$), radius, and diameter to provide more accurate estimates for specific graph classes[3][8].

### Related Concepts
*   **Vizing's Conjecture:** This famous conjecture relates the domination number of the Cartesian product of two graphs to the product of their individual domination numbers ($\gamma(G \Box H) \ge \gamma(G)\gamma(H)$)[9][10][11]. While distinct from diameter-based bounds, it is a central theme in domination theory that often appears alongside research into diameter-constrained graphs[12][13].
*   **Total Domination:** Similar studies exist for the total domination number ($\gamma_t(G)$), which also rely on graph structure, degrees, and diameter[14][15].

For specific applications or if you are working with a particular class of graphs (e.g., planar, chordal, or regular), researchers often use more specialized bounds that incorporate the graph's structural properties, as simple diameter-based bounds can be quite loose for general graphs[16].
