In graph theory, the **Roman domination number**, denoted by $\gamma_R(G)$, is a graph invariant related to resource allocation and network protection[1][2]. Research into this parameter often involves establishing bounds—both upper and lower—relative to other fundamental graph properties, including the **radius** ($\text{rad}(G)$) and **diameter** ($\text{diam}(G)$)[3][4][5].

### Relationship Between Parameters
*   **Definitions:** 
    *   The **diameter** of a graph $G$ is the maximum eccentricity of its vertices (the greatest distance between any two vertices)[6][2].
    *   The **radius** is the minimum eccentricity of the vertices in $G$[7][2].
*   **Bounding $\gamma_R(G)$:** Several studies seek to bound the Roman domination number using the diameter, girth, and order of the graph[3][4][5]. For instance, researchers have examined how these invariants interact in general graphs as well as in specific classes such as trees[3][8][5].
*   **Specific Results:** 
    *   For connected graphs with a diameter $\text{diam}(G) \geq 3$, there are theorems providing specific upper bounds for the Roman domination number, sometimes involving the complement of the graph[3].
    *   In the context of trees, the diameter is frequently used to establish structural properties that constrain the Roman domination number (e.g., if $\text{diam}(T) = 3$, specific bounds like $\gamma_R(T) \leq 4$ may apply)[9][8].
    *   Research often explores the interplay between $\gamma_R(G)$ and the radius/diameter alongside other parameters like maximum degree ($\Delta$) and minimum degree ($\delta$)[10][5].

If you are looking for a specific theorem or inequality, please note that these bounds are highly dependent on the graph class (e.g., general, trees, or regular graphs)[10][5]. Most academic literature on this topic focuses on proving that $\gamma_R(G)$ is constrained by the "distance" metrics of the graph to ensure efficient domination[3][11].
