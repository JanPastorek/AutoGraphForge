In graph theory, the **Roman domination number**, denoted by $\gamma_R(G)$, is a parameter that measures the minimum weight of a Roman dominating function on a graph $G$. The **diameter**, $diam(G)$, and **radius**, $rad(G)$, are fundamental distance-based parameters that frequently appear in bounds and relationships concerning domination numbers[1][2][3].

### Key Relationships and Bounds
While there is no single universal equality linking these three parameters for all graphs, they are related through various theoretical bounds and structural properties:

*   **General Distance Inequalities:** By definition, for any connected graph $G$, the radius and diameter are related by $rad(G) \leq diam(G) \leq 2 \cdot rad(G)$[4]. 
*   **Bounding $\gamma_R(G)$ with Diameter:** Research has explored upper bounds for the Roman domination number in terms of the diameter. For instance, it has been noted that for a graph $G$ with $\gamma_R(G) > 3$, the diameter can be bounded by a function of the Roman domination number, such as $diam(G) \leq \frac{3(\gamma_R(G) - 3)}{2} + 2$[5].
*   **Distance-based Domination:** The study of "distance Roman domination" often generalizes these concepts. The $k$-distance Roman domination number, $\gamma_{kR}(G)$, directly incorporates the distance properties of the graph, often involving $diam(G)$ in its definition or as a factor in its upper bounds (e.g., for certain graph classes)[6][7].
*   **Structural Context:** In many papers, $diam(G)$ and $rad(G)$ are used alongside $\gamma_R(G)$ to establish extremal results or to characterize specific graph families (like trees or grid graphs) where the Roman domination number can be constrained by the "span" or "depth" of the graph[8][9][10].

### Summary of Parameters
*   **Roman Domination Number ($\gamma_R(G)$):** The minimum weight of a function $f: V \to \{0, 1, 2\}$ such that every vertex with $f(u)=0$ is adjacent to at least one vertex with $f(v)=2$[1][11].
*   **Diameter ($diam(G)$):** The maximum distance between any two vertices in $G$[1][3].
*   **Radius ($rad(G)$):** The minimum eccentricity among all vertices in $G$, where the eccentricity of a vertex is the maximum distance to any other vertex[1][3].
