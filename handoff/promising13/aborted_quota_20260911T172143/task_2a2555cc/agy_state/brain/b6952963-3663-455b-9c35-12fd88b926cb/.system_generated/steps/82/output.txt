The inequality $\gamma_t(G) \ge \text{rad}(G)$ is a known lower bound for the total domination number of a connected graph $G$ with no isolated vertices[1][2][3]. 

While a single standard proof is often cited in graph theory literature (such as the work by DeLaViña et al. referenced in various surveys[1][2][3]), the phrasing "Let $S$ be a..." is the standard beginning for a proof by construction or contradiction involving a total dominating set.

### Context of the Inequality
*   **Definition:** For a connected graph $G$ with no isolated vertices, the total domination number $\gamma_t(G)$ is the minimum cardinality of a set $S \subseteq V(G)$ such that every vertex in $V(G)$ is adjacent to at least one vertex in $S$[4].
*   **The Bound:** The inequality $\gamma_t(G) \ge \text{rad}(G)$ relates the total domination number to the radius of the graph (the minimum eccentricity among all vertices)[5][2].
*   **Generalizations:** This result has been extended in various ways, including generalizations to the **total distance $k$-domination number** ($\gamma_{k}^t(G) \ge \text{rad}_k(G)$)[1][6][7].

### Typical Proof Approach
Proofs of this nature typically proceed by:
1.  **Selection:** Letting $S$ be a minimum total dominating set of $G$ (i.e., $|S| = \gamma_t(G)$)[8][9][10].
2.  **Exploitation of Structural Properties:** Using the fact that every vertex $v \in V(G)$ must have a neighbor in $S$, and every vertex $s \in S$ must have a neighbor in $S$[4][11].
3.  **Distance Arguments:** Utilizing the distance between vertices in $S$ and the remaining vertices in $V(G)$ to show that the size of $S$ must be at least the radius of the graph[1][2]. 

If you are looking for a specific proof text, it is likely found in foundational papers regarding total domination conjectures, such as those by **DeLaViña et al.** (often discussed in the context of the "Graffiti" computer program's conjectures)[1][12][13].
