The relationship between the **total domination number** ($\gamma_t(G)$) and the **radius** ($\text{rad}(G)$) of a graph is a recurring theme in the work of **Michael A. Henning** and his co-authors (notably in their research on distance domination and bounds for specific graph classes)[1][2][3][4].

### Key Context and Relevant Proof Approaches
In the literature authored by Henning et al., the radius $\text{rad}(G)$ is often used as a parameter to bound the total domination number, particularly for restricted classes of graphs (like planar graphs) or when considering distance-$k$ domination[1][3].

*   **Distance Domination and Radius:** A common proof strategy for lower bounds involving the radius involves the construction of a spanning tree $T$[1][2]. Because adding edges to a graph cannot increase its radius, one can establish that $\text{rad}(G) \le \text{rad}(T)$[1][2]. Since $T$ is a tree, the relationship between diameter, radius, and domination number is more easily calculated (e.g., using $\text{diam}(T) \ge 2\text{rad}(T) - 1$)[2].
*   **Planar Graphs:** Research by Dorfling, Goddard, and Henning[3][5] specifically explores how $\text{diam}(G)$ and $\text{rad}(G)$ influence $\gamma_t(G)$. For instance, they showed that for planar graphs with diameter 3, the upper bound for the total domination number varies depending on whether the radius is 2 or 3[3].

### Notable References
If you are looking for specific proofs, the following works by Henning are primary sources:

1.  **"Lower bounds on the distance domination number of a graph"** (with F. Kenter, *Contributions to Discrete Mathematics*, 2017): This paper contains proofs relating distance $k$-domination to the radius of the graph[1][4]. It explicitly uses the property that $\text{rad}(G) \le \text{rad}(T)$ for a spanning tree $T$[2].
2.  **"Total Domination in Graphs"** (Springer Monographs in Mathematics, 2013, with A. Yeo): This is the definitive text on the subject[6][7]. It provides the foundational theorems and proofs for various bounds on $\gamma_t(G)$, including those related to structural parameters like radius and diameter[8].
3.  **"A New Upper Bound on the Total Domination Number of a Graph"** (with A. Yeo, 2007): Focuses on bounds for graphs with specified minimum/maximum degrees[9].

To find the specific proof you are looking for, it is recommended to consult **Chapter 2 or 3 of the Henning & Yeo monograph**[7][8], or the **2017 *Contributions to Discrete Mathematics* paper**[1][4], which contains the most direct technical application of the radius parameter in a proof context.
