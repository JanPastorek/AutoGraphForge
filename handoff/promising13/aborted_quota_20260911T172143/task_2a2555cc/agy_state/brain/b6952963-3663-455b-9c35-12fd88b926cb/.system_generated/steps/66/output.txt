The terms "DeLaViña," "Larson," "Pepper," and "Waller" refer to a group of mathematicians—**Ermelinda DeLaViña, Craig E. Larson, Ryan Pepper, and Bill Waller**—who have co-authored several papers in graph theory[1][2][3].

Their research often involves the use of the computer program **Graffiti** (or **Graffiti.pc**) to generate and investigate conjectures in graph theory[1][3][4]. Regarding your specific keywords:

*   **Total Domination Number and Radius:** This research group has published work establishing various bounds for graph parameters[3][5]. Notably, it has been shown in their work (and cited in their papers) that the **total domination number** ($\gamma_t(G)$) of a simple connected graph is at least the **radius** ($r(G)$) of the graph[6][7][8].
*   **Research Context:** Much of their collaboration focuses on conjectures generated by Graffiti.pc, including bounds related to domination, total domination, independence numbers, and radius[3][9][4]. For example, they have published:
    *   "Some conjectures of Graffiti.pc on total domination" (*Congressus Numerantium*, 2007)[2][9].
    *   "On total domination and support vertices of a tree" (*AKCE International Journal of Graphs and Combinatorics*, 2010)[3][5].
    *   "Graffiti.pc on the 2-domination number of a graph" (*Congressus Numerantium*, 2010)[10][11].
    *   "A Characterization of Graphs Where the Independence Number Equals the Radius" (*Graphs and Combinatorics*, 2012)[12][13].

The phrase "total domination" appears frequently in their work as they analyze lower and upper bounds for this parameter in relation to other graph invariants like radius, average distance, and degree[3][14][15].
