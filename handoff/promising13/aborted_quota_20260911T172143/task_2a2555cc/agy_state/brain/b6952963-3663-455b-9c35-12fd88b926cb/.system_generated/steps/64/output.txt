Ermelinda DeLaViña and her colleagues have conducted significant research on the **total domination number** ($\gamma_t(G)$) of graphs, specifically establishing bounds related to the **radius** ($rad(G)$) of a graph[1][2].

### Key Findings
*   **Lower Bound Theorem:** DeLaViña et al. proved that for any connected graph $G$ with at least two vertices, the total domination number is at least the radius of the graph[1][2]:
    $$\gamma_t(G) \geq rad(G)$$
*   **Case of Equality:** Further research by the group provides a characterization for when this bound is met[1][2]. Specifically, for a connected graph $G$ and a total dominating set $S$ (where $|S| = \gamma_t(G)$), the equality $\gamma_t(G) = rad(G)$ holds if and only if the size of the subgraph induced by $S$, denoted $G[S]$, is $rad(G)/2$[1][2].
*   **Sharpness:** This bound is known to be sharp; for example, in the case of a path $P_n$ where $n \equiv 0 \pmod 4$, both the radius and the total domination number equal $n/2$[1][2].

### Research Context
These results are often cited in the context of graph theory research concerning domination parameters[1][2]. The work by DeLaViña, Craig Larson, Ryan Pepper, and Bill Waller frequently appears in literature related to extremal graph theory, particularly in studies involving trees and various domination invariants[3][4][5].
