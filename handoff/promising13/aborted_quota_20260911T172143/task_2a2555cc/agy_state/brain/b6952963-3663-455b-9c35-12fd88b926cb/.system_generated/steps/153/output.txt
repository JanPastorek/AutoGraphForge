The relationship between **total domination number**, **radius**, and **eccentricity** is a subject explored in graph theory, notably in the work of **Michael A. Henning and Anders Yeo**, particularly in their monograph *Total Domination in Graphs* (2013)[1][2][3].

### Key Concepts
*   **Total Domination Number ($\gamma_t(G)$):** The minimum cardinality of a set $S \subseteq V(G)$ such that every vertex in $G$ is adjacent to at least one vertex in $S$[4][3]. A total dominating set exists if and only if the graph has no isolated vertices[3].
*   **Eccentricity and Radius:** The eccentricity of a vertex $v$, denoted $ecc_G(v)$, is the maximum distance between $v$ and any other vertex in the graph. The **radius** of $G$, $rad(G)$, is the minimum eccentricity among all vertices of $G$[4][5][6].

### Relevant Bounds and Proofs
Research by Henning, Yeo, and others has established various lower bounds for the total domination number in terms of the radius of a graph[1][7].

*   **Radius Lower Bound:** For a connected graph $G$ of order $n \ge 2$ without isolated vertices, a known bound is:
    $$\gamma_t(G) \ge rad(G)$$
    This is often cited in literature discussing distance-based domination parameters[1][7].
*   **Generalizations:** This result has been generalized to the **total $k$-domination number** ($\gamma_{t,k}(G)$). Recent work (e.g., Davila et al.) provides a proof for the lower bound:
    $$\gamma_{t,k}(G) \ge \frac{1}{k} rad(G)$$
    The proof typically involves considering a spanning tree $T$ of $G$, noting that $rad(G) \le rad(T)$, and using the properties of trees and diameters to establish the inequality[1][8].

### Further Reading
*   **Monograph:** *Total Domination in Graphs* by Michael A. Henning and Anders Yeo (Springer Monographs in Mathematics, 2013) is the primary reference for these and other results concerning total domination[1][9][10].
*   **Papers:** Various papers by Haynes, Henning, and Yeo explore diameter-two critical graphs, bounds on domination parameters, and related topics in discrete mathematics[11][1].
