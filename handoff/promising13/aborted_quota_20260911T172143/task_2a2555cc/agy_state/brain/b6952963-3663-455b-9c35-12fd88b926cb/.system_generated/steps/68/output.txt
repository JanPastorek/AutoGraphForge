"Some conjectures of Graffiti.pc on total domination" is a research paper published in **Congressus Numerantium (Volume 185, 2007, pp. 81–95)** by **Ermelinda DeLaViña, Qi Liu, Ryan Pepper, Bill Waller, and Douglas B. West**[1][2][3].

### Overview
The paper presents and investigates several lower bounds on the **total domination number** of a graph that originated as conjectures from **Graffiti.pc**, a computer program developed by Ermelinda DeLaViña for automated conjecture generation in graph theory[4][5]. The research provides mathematical proofs for many of these conjectures, which often relate the total domination number to other graph invariants such as:
*   **Radius ($r(G)$):** One of the paper's key findings is that the total domination number of a graph is at least its radius[6].
*   **Degree and Connectivity:** The work explores how the total domination number relates to parameters like maximum degree and the number of cut-vertices[7].
*   **Girth ($g(G)$):** The paper discusses specific conjectures, such as one suggesting that for a connected graph $G$ that is not a tree, the total domination number $t(G) \geq (g(G)+1)/\delta'(G)$ (where $\delta'(G)$ is the minimum degree)[8].

### Context in Graph Theory
*   **Automated Conjecturing:** The paper is part of a broader body of work in the field of automated discovery, where software like Graffiti and its successor, Graffiti.pc, are used to identify non-trivial inequalities between graph invariants[9][5].
*   **Related Work:** The same research group has published extensively on related invariants, including the standard domination number, independence number, and their relationships with the radius of a graph[2][10][11]. For instance, a related paper by some of the same authors in *Discussiones Mathematicae Graph Theory* (2010) similarly examines lower bounds for the standard domination number, noting that it is at least two-thirds of the radius[2][12].

The paper serves as a significant reference in the study of total domination and remains frequently cited in research concerning automated conjecturing and distance-based graph invariants[13][10].
