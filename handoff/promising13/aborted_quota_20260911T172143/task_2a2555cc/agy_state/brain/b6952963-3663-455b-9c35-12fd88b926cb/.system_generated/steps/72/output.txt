The research and conjectures regarding "total domination" and "radius" hosted on Douglas West's website primarily relate to his work with the conjecturing program **Graffiti.pc**[1] and his collaborative research on graph theory[2][3].

### Key References on Douglas West's Website
*   **Graffiti.pc Conjectures:** Douglas West's page on "Some Conjectures of Graffiti.pc (2004-07)" lists several conjectures relating the total domination number $\gamma_t(G)$ to the radius $r(G)$ and the minimum degree $\delta(G)$[1]. For example:
    *   One conjecture suggests that if $G$ is connected, then $\gamma_t(G) \geq 2r(G)/\delta(G)$[1].
*   **Corrections:** His "Introduction to Graph Theory - corrections" page includes references to exercise clarifications involving the term "radius"[4].
*   **Collaborative Research:** West is a co-author of papers—such as "Some Conjectures of Graffiti.pc on Total Domination"—that explore these relationships as part of a broader study of domination parameters[2][3].

### Related Mathematical Context
In the broader graph theory literature, the relationship between the total domination number $\gamma_t(G)$ and the radius $r(G)$ is a well-studied topic[5][6][7]:
*   **Lower Bounds:** It is known that for a connected graph $G$ of order at least two, the radius provides a lower bound for the total domination number (often cited as $\gamma_t(G) \geq \text{rad}(G)$ in specific contexts or classes of graphs)[8][9].
*   **Total Domination Polynomials:** Research also exists on the roots of total domination polynomials, where the radius of the circle containing these roots is defined in terms of the graph's order $n$ and minimum degree $\delta$[10][11].
