The study of the **total domination number** ($\gamma_t(G)$) in relation to graph parameters like the **radius** ($rad(G)$) is a well-established area in graph theory[1][2]. Research often focuses on establishing lower bounds for $\gamma_t(G)$ using the radius, diameter, and other structural properties[3][2][4].

### Key Concepts and Findings
*   **Lower Bounds:** Numerous studies have sought to bound the total domination number in terms of the radius[1][2]. For instance, results have been generalized to the "total $k$-domination number," providing lower bounds that utilize the radius of a connected graph[1][3].
*   **Conjectures and Counterexamples:** The field frequently employs "conjecturing programs" (such as those associated with the *Graffiti* software) to generate potential relationships between graph invariants[5][6][7]. These programs often produce conjectures that are then tested; when a conjecture fails, researchers identify **counterexamples** to refine the bounds[8][7].
*   **Automated Discovery:** Tools like *AutoGraphForge* have been used to automate the discovery of such bounds and the search for counterexamples, helping to classify which graphs satisfy specific equalities (e.g., where the domination number equals the total domination number)[9][10].
*   **Standard Relationships:** It is known that for any graph $G$ without isolated vertices, the domination number $\gamma(G)$ and total domination number $\gamma_t(G)$ are related by $\gamma(G) \leq \gamma_t(G) \leq 2\gamma(G)$[9]. Bounds involving the radius often aim to sharpen these inequalities for specific classes of graphs (like trees or planar graphs)[9][11][12].

### Relevant Literature
If you are looking for specific proofs or counterexamples, the following areas are commonly cited in the literature:
*   **Total Domination in Trees:** Research often explores how the radius and support vertices influence $\gamma_t(G)$ specifically for trees[11][13][14].
*   **Diameter and Radius Interaction:** Many bounds are improved when the diameter is constrained relative to the radius (e.g., cases where the diameter is small, such as $diam(G) = 2$ or $3$)[15][9].
*   **Research Databases:** Papers discussing these bounds are frequently found in journals such as *Discrete Mathematics*, *Journal of Graph Theory*, and through research archives like *arXiv* or *ResearchGate*[3][6][16].
