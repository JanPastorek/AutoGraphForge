In the monograph *Total Domination in Graphs* (2013) by Michael A. Henning and Anders Yeo, the authors provide several fundamental bounds relating the total domination number, $\gamma_t(G)$, to other graph invariants, including the radius of a graph, $\text{rad}(G)$[1][2].

Specifically, they state the following result:

**Proposition:** If $G$ is a connected graph of order at least two, then $\gamma_t(G) \ge \text{rad}(G)$[1].

### Context and Related Bounds
The book serves as a primary reference for total domination theory[3][2]. In addition to the inequality involving the radius, the text includes several related lower bounds for the total domination number:

*   **Diameter:** For a connected graph $G$ of order at least two, $\gamma_t(G) \ge \frac{\text{diam}(G) + 1}{2}$[1].
*   **Girth:** For a graph with girth $g$, $\gamma_t(G) \ge g/2$[1].
*   **Domination Number:** For a graph with no isolated vertices, $\gamma(G) \le \gamma_t(G) \le 2\gamma(G)$[1][4].

The proofs for these and other results are contained within the technical sections of the monograph, which covers both algorithmic aspects and structural graph theory related to total domination[5][2]. For the formal proof of the radius bound, readers are typically referred to the specific chapter addressing lower bounds in the *Total Domination in Graphs* text[1][2].
