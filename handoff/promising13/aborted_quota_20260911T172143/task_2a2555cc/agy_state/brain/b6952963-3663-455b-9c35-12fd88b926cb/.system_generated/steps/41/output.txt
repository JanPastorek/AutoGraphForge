In graph theory, the relationship between the **radius** ($\text{rad}(G)$) and the **total domination number** ($\gamma_t(G)$) of a graph $G$ is a subject of ongoing research, focusing on establishing bounds that link these parameters[1][2].

### Key Concepts
*   **Total Domination Number ($\gamma_t(G)$):** The minimum cardinality of a set $S \subseteq V(G)$ such that every vertex in the graph is adjacent to at least one vertex in $S$[1][3]. Unlike the standard domination number, this requires every vertex to have a neighbor in the dominating set, meaning the graph cannot have isolated vertices[4][3][5].
*   **Radius ($\text{rad}(G)$):** The minimum eccentricity among all vertices in the graph, where the eccentricity of a vertex is the maximum distance to any other vertex in the graph[2][6][7].

### Relationship and Bounds
Research into the interplay between these two parameters often involves:
*   **Lower and Upper Bounds:** Because finding the total domination number is an NP-hard problem, researchers frequently look for tight bounds based on distance metrics like the radius and diameter[1][8]. For instance, bounds have been developed that generalize distance-based domination frameworks[1].
*   **Comparison Studies:** Some studies compare the total domination number with other parameters (such as the Roman domination number or distance-2 domination number) specifically relative to the radius of certain graph classes (e.g., trapezoid graphs or trees)[2][9].
*   **Radius-Forcing Sets:** There is also work concerning "radius-forcing sets," where the radius of a graph is determined by a specific subset of vertices, intersecting with total domination theory[4].
*   **Structural Influence:** Results often highlight that restrictions on the radius or diameter can force specific bounds on $\gamma_t(G)$[10]. For example, in specific families of graphs, the total domination number can be constrained or characterized by the central vertices (vertices with minimum eccentricity)[11].

If you are looking for a specific theorem or inequality relating the two, it is often expressed in the context of specific graph families (like trees or regular graphs) where the relationship becomes more predictable[1].
