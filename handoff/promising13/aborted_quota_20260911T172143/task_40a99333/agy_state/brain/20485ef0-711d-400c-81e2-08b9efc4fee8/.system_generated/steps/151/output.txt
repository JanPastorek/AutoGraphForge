The search for ""total domination number" "at most the Roman domination number" OR "total domination number is at most the Roman domination number"" returned the following summary:
The relationship between the total domination number, denoted by $\gamma_t(G)$, and the Roman domination number, denoted by $\gamma_R(G)$, is a known result in graph theory. Specifically, for any graph $G$ (with no isolated vertices to ensure the total domination number is well-defined), the following inequality holds:

$$\gamma_t(G) \le \gamma_R(G)$$

Research indicates that the Roman domination number is often greater than or equal to the total domination number[1][2][3]. This inequality is frequently cited in studies exploring bounds for various domination parameters in graphs[3]. 

It is worth noting that while $\gamma_t(G) \le \gamma_R(G)$, these parameters are distinct from others like the weak Roman domination number, which may be incomparable to the total domination number[1].
